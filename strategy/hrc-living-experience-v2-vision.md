- **Source of truth:** *HRC // Strategic Playbook — The Living Experience* (31 pp., Oct 2026), treated as **V1**. Page references are `p.N`.
- **Method:** a synthetic council of ten lenses: product and aesthetics, emotional brand, adaptive systems, motion, behaviour, commercial, creative technology, AI-native, platform, and a skeptic. It speaks with one voice, and disagreements are recorded where they matter.
- **Labels used in this document**
  - **[V1]** an idea taken from the playbook
  - **[NEW]** an idea introduced here
  - **[ASSUMPTION]** must be tested before HRC commits to it
- **Scope:** this is not a redesign. Example copy is illustrative, and any durations are proposals to tune in prototypes.

## 1. Executive Assessment

**Verdict: the concept is fundamentally strong.** Keep its spine:
- the site as proof (p.2)
- the evidence graph as the moat (p.5)
- the DOM / semantic product first (p.16)
- the evidence gate (p.21)
- privacy-first adaptation (p.19)

**The core weakness: V1 is a museum of HRC's transformations.**
- The visitor is a spectator for five of the six journey steps.
- Their own business only appears at "Assemble Reset" (p.13).
- Deconstruct, X-Ray, Rewind and Why all operate on HRC's *past* work.

**The biggest unrealized opportunity: make the visitor's business the material.**
- Don't *show* a Hard Reset. Let the visitor *run* one, on their own company, in miniature, within about two minutes. Then show the proof that HRC has done it for real.

**Lens 01's question, "website or new category?", answered: a new category.**
- Software companies let you try before you buy. Professional-services firms say "contact us".
- HRC can be the services firm whose site is a **working trial of its thinking**: a *diagnostic interface*.
- That is "I haven't seen a professional-services company do that before."

**A second unrealized opportunity: one object across the whole client relationship.**
- The Reset object can run from first visit → discovery call → proposal → delivery → case study.
- The site then becomes HRC's operating system, not its brochure.

**The biggest risk: being admired instead of hired.**
- Every V2 change trades spectacle for usefulness *without* giving up distinctiveness.

**A naming flag:**
- The playbook says "Hard Reset **Design**" (p.1); this brief says "Hard Reset **Co.**".
- Settle the name before any identity work.

**Decisions only HRC can make (V2 depends on them):**
1. The primary buyer. This is still open in V1 (p.8).
2. Whether to offer a fixed-scope paid diagnostic, and whether its price is public.
3. Whether a named practitioner will reply to every *qualified* Reset ("The Reply"), and within what promised response time.
4. Whether to add a case-study / evidence-rights clause to client contracts.
5. Whether to build the public-site Scan, and the legal and abuse posture that comes with it.
6. The final company name.

## 3. Where V1 Is Too Safe

- **The spectator problem.**
  - All signature moments act on HRC's past work.
  - Personalisation means *re-ranking proof* (p.13), the safest possible form of adaptation.
- **Your Hard Reset is an endpoint.** It is step 5 of 6 in the journey (p.13) and is built in Phase 4 (p.23). It should be the spine of the experience.
- **Intent capture is a question, not a mirror.**
  - V1: "Ask what the visitor is trying to change" (p.13).
  - Stronger: *show* visitors their likely system and let them correct it.
- **There are no humans.** The playbook never mentions the people who do the work: no founders, no practitioners, no voices. Services are bought from people.
- **The emotional range is purely intellectual.**
  - V1's targets are "command, curiosity, intelligence" (p.26), "authority, craft" (p.27) and "uncanny relevance" (p.28).
  - "Uncanny" literally means unsettling.
  - Missing: **relief, warmth, humour, vulnerability**.
- **The "before" isn't designed.**
  - A transformation only lands if you *feel* the mess first.
  - V1 opens with components already converging (p.10), so the tension is resolved before it's felt.
- **The evidence stops at launch day.**
  - There's no "does it still hold 12 months later?"
  - There are no honest failures beyond "discarded alternatives" (p.10).
- **Transparency stops at case studies.** HRC's own site, response times and tracking can't be inspected, despite "Make trust a system" (p.21).
- **Learning means optimising the site only** (p.20).
  - Thousands of declared business problems add up to market intelligence.
  - V1 ignores it.
- **The commercial model is thin.**
  - There is one conversion and no productised entry offer.
  - "Three disqualifiers" are mentioned (p.8) but never shown to visitors.
- **Labs is a sandbox** when it could be a public R&D record.
- **Audio and haptics** are flagged as a gap (p.7) and never designed.
- **Machine visitors are ignored.**
  - V1 designs only for humans in browsers.
  - In 2026, buyers also research through AI assistants and answer engines.

## 4. Where V1 Is Too Ambitious

- **The Orbital 3D shell.**
  - It applies spatial navigation to content that isn't spatial, and the "constellation" metaphor is arbitrary.
  - It is the lowest-scoring option in V1's own matrix: 18/25 (p.29), with 3/5 on mobile.
- **Four custom 3D (WebGL) interactions in one six-week phase** (p.23): Hard Reset, Deconstruct, X-Ray and Rewind.
- **Three art directions and three palettes** (p.26–28).
- **Seven depth levels** (p.10), against four-step progressive disclosure elsewhere (p.6).
- **AI-composed interfaces in Phase 4 with one flagship case** (p.23–24). Composing "personalised" sequences from a single case is theatre.
- **Two decisions before any value:** the boot sequence, then a choice of mode (p.10).
- **The team and governance.**
  - An eight-role Experience Core, with weekly, monthly and quarterly governance (p.21).
  - That is probably sized beyond a small studio **[ASSUMPTION]**.
- **WebGPU evaluation** (p.16). Fine to watch, but it adds nothing to the roadmap.
- **"The site listens" (Sentient Signal).**
  - Reshaping the interface in real time is expensive to get right.
  - It risks feeling covert, which is V1's own high-level privacy risk (p.25).

### The Skeptic's ledger
- **Sounds brilliant, won't work:** "Explore / Spatial" as a mode. Most B2B visitors want to *judge*, not explore. Free-roaming navigation is homework.
- **Exists because designers would enjoy building it:**
  - Rewind as a standalone interaction
  - film grain, bloom and orbit physics
  - the aubergine-and-magenta Signal world
- **Novelty posing as strategy:** "the site listens." Listens to what? With one case, there's nothing to adapt between.
- **Where prospects get confused:**
  - mode names ("Explore" vs. "Guided")
  - the jargon category "transformation architecture"
  - the word "reset" itself. Owners may fear *destruction* of their brand or data. V1's motion grammar says "Deconstruct = disclosure, not destruction" (p.15); the copy must say it too.
- **Where performance fails:**
  - WebGL on a mid-tier Android over 4G
  - an intro animation that delays the main content appearing (LCP)
  - AI response time on the critical path
- **Where AI turns gimmicky:**
  - a named guide persona
  - generated prose about client results
  - adaptation that feels "uncanny"
- **Where it becomes self-indulgent:** any stretch where HRC talks about HRC for longer than it talks about the visitor.
- **Assumptions without evidence:**
  - buyers want to explore
  - 3D increases credibility
  - prospects will build a Reset
  - traffic is high enough to support personalisation and mode comparison
  - the target buyer likes a dark-neon aesthetic
  - HRC has enough evidence
  - owner-led companies respond to "systems" language
- **What makes a sophisticated buyer leave:**
  - there are no humans on the site
  - they can't tell what an engagement looks like or roughly costs
  - claims come without numbers
  - the site is slow on a phone
  - cleverness comes before clarity
  - there's nothing they can forward to their boss
  - the whole site is about HRC

## 5. The Five Biggest Strategic Unlocks

### Unlock 1 — Flip the subject: the visitor's business is the material [NEW]
- **The shift:** from "watch HRC reset other companies" to "watch *your* company reset, then see proof HRC has done it before."
- **How it works:**
  - Within 30–60 seconds, the visitor picks symptoms (or optionally enters their URL).
  - The site draws their *likely* system.
  - All proof is then linked to their system: "this break in your system → where we fixed the same break."
- **Why it's material:**
  - Recognition ("they understand my company") is the strongest trust trigger for an owner.
  - Proof becomes relevant by construction.
  - Diagnostic data is captured by the act of exploring itself.
- **What it requires:** a curated map from symptom → cause → capability → evidence. This map is HRC's real intellectual property.
- **Risk:**
  - Visitors may not want to do work.
  - Mitigation: show a demo that needs no input first, make every input optional, and make value visible after the first click.

### Unlock 2 — Your Hard Reset becomes a lifecycle product (the Reset File) [NEW]
- **The shift:** from a CTA to one durable object that runs: site → discovery call → proposal → kickoff → delivery → verified case study.
- **Why it's material:**
  - Discovery calls start 20 minutes in, because the context is already there.
  - Proposals inherit its structure; delivery inherits its diagnosis.
  - The case study inherits a *documented* "before" state.
  - One object produces sales intelligence, delivery efficiency *and* evidence.
- **What it requires:**
  - a Reset File schema
  - sync with the CRM and DBR/Nexus
  - versioning
- **Risk:** scope creep. V1 launch ships only the pre-sale stages (§7.5).

### Unlock 3 — The site proves itself: "Case Study Zero", live [NEW]
- **The shift:** any visitor can X-ray the actual page they're on and see:
  - its components and content objects
  - its live performance and accessibility tree
  - **what the site knows about them**
- **Why it's material:**
  - A technical buyer can audit HRC's craft on the spot.
  - It also *forces* discipline: you can't expose a messy stack, so the feature keeps HRC's own house in order.
- **What it requires:**
  - clean architecture
  - metadata in the markup
  - minimal third-party scripts (14 marketing trackers would destroy the story)
- **Risk:** it exposes imperfections. That is the point; publish the improvements.

### Unlock 4 — The evidence flywheel: delivery produces the proof [NEW]
- **The shift:**
  - Every engagement emits decisions, artifacts and outcomes as part of the delivery itself.
  - Publication rights are agreed *at contract time*, in tiers: named, anonymised, or structural only.
  - Outcomes are re-checked at 3, 6 and 12 months.
- **Why it's material:**
  - It solves V1's largest dependency: one flagship case with unsecured rights (p.24–25).
  - It makes possible a credibility claim almost no agency can make: "result still holding at 12 months."
- **What it requires:**
  - changes to the delivery templates
  - a contract clause
  - an owner for evidence
- **Risk:** clients refuse to be named. That's why the publication tiers exist.

### Unlock 5 — A value ladder with a productised first step [NEW]
- **The shift:** from one conversion point to a ladder:
  1. Save your Reset
  2. Send it and get a human reply
  3. Book a call
  4. A **fixed-scope paid Reset Diagnostic**
  5. An engagement
  6. An ongoing "Living System" retainer
- **Why it's material:**
  - Every level of confidence has a next step.
  - A paid diagnostic is the strongest qualification signal there is.
  - Resets that span several layers show the buyer a *connected system* rather than one deliverable, which should raise average opportunity value **[ASSUMPTION — test it]**.
- **What it requires:** offer design, a pricing decision (HRC's call) and capacity.
- **Risk:** the diagnostic becomes a commodity. Keep it high-touch and named.

## 6. Signature Experience Candidates

Each candidate is judged on six tests: **memorable, meaningful, useful, repeatable, technically defensible, commercially relevant.**

### Candidate 1 — HOLD TO RESET [NEW]
- **User action:**
  - Press and hold one control, the way you hold a device's power button to hard-reset it.
  - Keyboard: hold Space or Enter, or press once to reset instantly.
  - Screen readers: a standard button that resets instantly.
- **System response:**
  - A ring fills over about 1.2–1.5 seconds while the tangled system tenses (lines straighten, noise quiets).
  - On completion: about 150 ms of stillness (the "black screen" of a reboot), then the system **rebuilds from infrastructure up to brand**.
  - Releasing early cancels, with a gentle spring back.
- **Emotional effect:** agency and relief. The visitor *causes* the transformation; the hold builds anticipation, and the beat of stillness makes them catch their breath.
- **Information communicated:**
  - the structure before and after
  - what changed (redundancies removed, connections made)
  - HRC's philosophy: *foundations first*
- **Commercial value:**
  - It's the moment that turns symptoms into a hypothesis.
  - It becomes HRC's recognisable act, usable on the home demo, on cases, on the visitor's own model, even on the 404 page.
- **Technical approach:**
  - DOM / SVG, with two layouts of the same nodes animated between them (FLIP or the View Transitions API).
  - A small spring-physics module; no WebGL.
  - An ARIA live region announces the result, e.g. "Reset complete: 14 handoffs reduced to 6."
  - Reduced motion shows the same story as stills.
- **Skeptic:**
  - A gesture alone is a gimmick. It only earns its place when the before→after mapping is real (Candidate 2).
  - Holding can be hard for people with some motor impairments, so the instant path must always exist. Releasing to cancel follows WCAG 2.5.2 Pointer Cancellation.

### Candidate 2 — THE TANGLE (symptom → system) [NEW; evolves "Guided", p.10]
- **User action:**
  - Pick symptoms in plain language, for example "Leads go cold after the first call", "We type the same data into three tools", "Our site doesn't feel like us anymore".
  - Or type your own.
- **System response:**
  - Each symptom adds nodes and links to a live map of the visitor's *probable* current system, across the five layers.
  - Root causes surface, for example "these three symptoms usually share one cause: no single customer record."
  - Each inference carries a confidence label; unknowns appear as open brackets.
- **Emotional effect:** recognition ("that's us"), then the relief of being *explained*.
- **Information communicated:**
  - causal links across layers, such as a brand problem that is really a data problem
  - it teaches HRC's systems thinking by demonstration
- **Commercial value:**
  - the diagnosis doubles as qualification
  - it maps to offers and seeds the Reset File
  - aggregated, it becomes market intelligence (§15)
- **Technical approach:**
  - A rules engine over a curated symptom map. Start with about 30–40 symptoms **[ASSUMPTION]**.
  - An SVG layout.
  - Optional AI maps free text to a symptom and *asks the visitor to confirm the match*.
  - An accessible equivalent: a structured list of symptom → likely cause → layer → confidence.
- **Skeptic:**
  - The quality of the symptom map is everything. One wrong inference destroys the "they understand me" moment.
  - Every inference shows its reasoning and can be dismissed.

### Candidate 3 — X-RAY THIS PAGE [EVOLVES V1 X-Ray, p.10]
- **User action:** toggle X-Ray (the X key or a button) on any page, including the one being read.
- **System response:**
  - The surface flips to Void, and components outline themselves with their names.
  - Content blocks show their object type and evidence status.
  - A panel shows:
    - this session's live Core Web Vitals and the page weight
    - the accessibility tree
    - the analytics events fired (only if the visitor consented)
    - "**everything this site knows about you**", with a delete button
- **Emotional effect:** trust through exposure, delight for technical buyers. "They have nothing to hide."
- **Information communicated:** HRC's architectural discipline, privacy posture and performance. Proof of craft with no case study needed.
- **Commercial value:**
  - converts technical evaluators
  - very shareable among developers
  - can't be faked cheaply by an agency running on templates
- **Technical approach:**
  - data attributes in the markup
  - `PerformanceObserver` / the web-vitals library
  - a consent log
  - a DOM overlay (no WebGL)
  - cheap to build; demanding to stay disciplined
- **Skeptic:**
  - It could overwhelm non-technical visitors, so it's off by default and promoted through the Technical lens.
  - It will reveal flaws; treat that as public accountability.

### Candidate 4 — THE DECISION TRAIL [MERGES V1 Rewind + Why, p.10]
- **User action:** scrub a case file's timeline from launch back to kickoff; open "the road not taken" at any decision point.
- **System response:**
  - The artifact visibly reverts; it stays the same object as the timeline moves.
  - The branch view shows the alternative, why it was rejected, and the trade-off.
  - The practitioner's note appears in their own words.
- **Emotional effect:** respect for HRC's judgement, and closeness: "this is how they think."
- **Information communicated:** trade-offs, reasoning, awareness of risk. This is what clients actually pay for.
- **Commercial value:**
  - credibility on complex, high-value deals
  - it kills the "screenshot gallery" problem
- **Technical approach:**
  - The case is stored as an ordered decision graph.
  - The scrubber is a native range input, which works by keyboard and meets WCAG 2.5.7 Dragging Movements.
  - Each state gets its own URL, with assets preloaded per decision.
- **Skeptic:** it needs real decision logs captured during delivery (Unlock 4). Defer until at least two cases have them.

### Candidate 5 — THE ROLE LENS [NEW]
- **User action:** switch lens between Owner, Marketing and Technical, or choose "Send to my CFO".
- **System response:**
  - The same page re-weights itself:
    - the owner sees outcome, risk, time and money
    - marketing sees brand and funnel
    - technical sees architecture and integration
  - Nothing is hidden; it's re-ordered and re-summarised, and objects keep their identity as they move.
- **Emotional effect:** "this speaks my language."
- **Information communicated:** one truth at three altitudes. That demonstrates stakeholder alignment, which is itself something HRC sells.
- **Commercial value:**
  - aligns the buying committee
  - gives each role its own share link
  - reduces the champion's internal-selling burden
- **Technical approach:**
  - role-tagged summaries for each object, drafted with AI and *approved by a human*
  - rendered by fixed rules
  - the lens is a URL parameter
- **Skeptic:** it triples the summaries to write. Start with the flagship case and the Reset File only.

### Candidate 6 — THE SCAN (your public surface, deconstructed) [NEW]
- **User action:** optionally enter a company URL.
- **System response:**
  - In about 30–60 seconds, the visitor's homepage appears deconstructed into the five layers, with factual observations:
    - measured performance from PageSpeed Insights, and CrUX field data *where the site has enough traffic to have any*
    - automatically detectable accessibility issues
    - detected CMS, analytics and other tags
    - whether metadata and structured data are present
  - Every line is labelled **FACT** (measured), **INFERENCE** or **UNKNOWN**, for example "We can't see your CRM from outside — tell us."
- **Emotional effect:** *"They understand my company before I've spoken with them."* This is the owner reaction the brief asks for.
- **Information communicated:** an honest, specific, non-generic picture of the visitor's public-facing system.
- **Commercial value:**
  - the highest-intent entry point
  - seeds the Reset File with facts
  - sales gets a teardown before the first call
- **Technical approach:**
  - a server-side headless browser
  - the PageSpeed Insights and CrUX APIs
  - technology fingerprinting
  - AI summarisation constrained to the measured facts
  - **security:** guard against server-side request forgery, respect robots.txt, rate-limit and add bot protection
  - **scanned content is untrusted input**, so it must be defended against prompt injection
  - results are private by default
- **Skeptic:**
  - it can feel invasive or judgmental
  - every scan costs money
  - competitors and bots can abuse it
  - automated accessibility tools catch only part of what WCAG covers, so it must never declare a site "accessible" or "inaccessible"
  - **Start as a concierge service:** a person produces the first scans by hand to learn which observations actually matter, then automate those.

### Candidate 7 — THE REPLY [NEW]
- **User action:** send the Reset File.
- **System response:**
  - An immediate, honest acknowledgement that states the response window.
  - Within that window, which HRC sets, a *named* practitioner annotates the visitor's Reset File in their view:
    - what they agree with
    - what they'd challenge
    - one question
    - one suggested next step
    - signed with their name
- **Emotional effect:** being seen by a human, and genuine reciprocity.
- **Information communicated:** HRC's judgement applied to *their* case, plus the people behind the work.
- **Commercial value:**
  - strong conversion from reply to call **[ASSUMPTION]**
  - the annotated File becomes the discovery agenda
- **Technical approach:**
  - an annotation layer on the Reset File
  - a CRM task with a deadline
  - a notification email
  - technically simple
- **Skeptic:**
  - It costs staff time for every lead.
  - Limit it to visitors who fit (honest disqualification comes first) and cap the volume.
  - **If HRC can't keep the promised response time, don't launch it.**

## 7. The Evolved "Your Hard Reset"

### 7.1 What it is
- A **private, living model of the visitor's business transformation**:
  - built gradually as they explore
  - owned by the visitor and portable
  - **valuable even if they never hire HRC**
- **One noun, three verbs:**
  - "Start your Hard Reset" → "Add to your Hard Reset" → "Send your Hard Reset"
  - This replaces the four names V1 uses: Build My Hard Reset, Your Hard Reset, Build My Reset, and audit/conversation (p.9, p.13, p.28).
- **Internal object name:** the Reset File.

### 7.2 Twelve dimensions folded into V1's five layers

| Layer (V1, p.10) | Dimensions |
|---|---|
| Brand | Brand · Content · Marketing |
| Interface | Website · Customer journey · Commerce |
| Data | CRM · Data · Analytics |
| Automation & Intelligence | Automation · AI |
| Infrastructure | Infrastructure (hosting, integrations, security) |

- Five layers keep the cognitive load low and match the Deconstruct vocabulary used everywhere else.

### 7.3 Every entry carries an epistemic label

| Label | Meaning in the Reset File | Who can assign it | Visual mark (not colour alone) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FACT** | Measured (Scan), or stated by the visitor; the source is shown as "measured" or "you said" | System, visitor | Solid ink |
| **EVIDENCE** | A linked HRC proof object: "We've seen this before → [case]" | Evidence graph | Citation tick |
| **INFERENCE** | Derived by HRC's rules from facts, with the reasoning shown | Rules engine (AI only with labels) | Dashed outline |
| **RECOMMENDATION** | What HRC suggests for this layer, and why | Rules, then a human | Lime fill behind ink |
| **HYPOTHESIS** | The assembled Reset as a whole: testable and editable | System, then visitor and HRC | Hatched |
| **UNKNOWN** | Required information that is missing; becomes a discovery question | System | Open bracket `[ ]` |

- **Unknowns are a feature, not a gap.** They *are* the discovery-call agenda.

### 7.4 How it gets built
- **Symptoms** (the Tangle): the core input.
- **"+ Add to your Hard Reset"** on any case, capability or decision.
- **Suggestions, never silent additions:** "You spent time on CRM work. Add CRM to your Reset?"
- **An optional URL Scan** (V2).
- **Optional context**, such as team size, role or timing. Ask only when the answer *unlocks* something, e.g. "To show a relevant engagement shape, tell us your team size."

### 7.5 Outputs, staged by release

| Output | What it is | Release |
|---|---|---|
| Reset Summary | On-screen and editable: layers, symptoms, evidence, unknowns | **V1 launch** |
| Diagnostic summary | Symptoms → likely causes, with labels | **V1 launch** |
| Private saved link | Saved via a magic link sent by email; no password | **V1 launch** |
| One-page executive summary (PDF) | Shareable with the buying committee | **V1 launch** |
| Discovery-call briefing | Internal, for HRC | **V1 launch** |

- **Not in V1:**
  - architectures generated freely by AI
  - price estimates
  - accounts and passwords
  - real-time co-editing

### 7.6 Lifecycle (each stage maps to a CRM stage)
`Empty → Seeded → Building → Reset (hypothesis) → Saved → Sent → Replied → Call → Diagnostic → Engagement → Verified case`

### 7.7 Privacy and ownership
- It lives in the browser until the visitor chooses to save or send it.
- Before sending, a **"What HRC will see"** preview shows exactly what leaves the browser.
- There's no email wall to start or view it. Email is needed only to save or send.
- It can be deleted at any time, and the retention period is stated.

### 7.8 Skeptic
- **"We're giving the diagnosis away."**
  - Some visitors will take it elsewhere. Accept that.
  - Generosity *is* the brand, and a hypothesis isn't the execution.
- **"Completion will be low."**
  - Design for value at *one* symptom, not twelve.
  - Measure Reset starts, sends and quality, not completeness.

## 9. Visual + Emotional Direction

### 9.1 Challenging the emotional sequence
- **The proposed sequence:** Curiosity → Recognition → Surprise → Understanding → Credibility → Possibility → Personal relevance → Desire → Action.
- **What's wrong with it:**
  - **Curiosity first is wrong for B2B.** Recognition hooks faster and more reliably than intrigue.
  - **Personal relevance arrives 7th.** It should be 2nd. That's Unlock 1.
  - **There's no tension before the possibility.** A transformation needs a felt "before".
  - **"Desire" is incomplete for B2B.** Buyers need *safety*: the confidence to justify the choice to others.
  - **It ends at action.** What happens *after* the action decides whether they follow through.
- **V2 sequence:**
  - INTRIGUE: "this isn't like the others."
  - RECOGNITION: "that's us."
  - TENSION: "and it's costing us."
  - RELIEF: "it can be untangled — I just saw it happen."
  - UNDERSTANDING: "I see *why* it's tangled."
  - CONVICTION: "they've done this, and they think well."
  - OWNERSHIP: "this is *my* Reset."
  - MOMENTUM: "the next step is small and clear."
  - ANTICIPATION: "I know what happens next, and when."

### 9.2 The first 5 seconds, 30 seconds, 2 minutes, 10 minutes
- **5 seconds — Intrigue + calm confidence:** "I know what this is, and it isn't like the others."
  - Fast, quiet, legible. No spectacle.
- **30 seconds — Recognition + tension:** "They're describing my mess."
- **2 minutes — Relief + agency:** "I just watched my business come back together, and *I* did it."
- **10 minutes — Conviction + ownership + momentum:** "They've done this before, they think well, and I know my next step."

### 9.3 State map

| State | What the user sees | What they understand | What they feel | What they can do next |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Arrival** (0–5 s) | One sentence in large type (illustrative: *"Your business isn't broken. It's disconnected."*), a quietly tangled system, one control | HRC fixes how businesses *connect*, not just how they look | Intrigue, calm | Hold to reset · Start with your problem · scroll |
| **Demonstration** (5–30 s) | The demo tangle resets: chaos → a structure built foundation-first | HRC rebuilds from the foundation up | Surprise, satisfaction | "Now do yours →" · see a real case |
| **Mirror** (Tangle, 30 s–2 min) | Their own symptoms become their probable system, with causes labelled | Their symptoms are *connected*; there's likely one root cause | Recognition, tension, then relief at being explained | Add, remove, or correct · Hold to reset |
| **The Reset** (~2 min) | Their system rebuilt, with a diff: fewer handoffs, one customer record, etc. | What a Hard Reset would change for *them* (a hypothesis) | Agency, relief, possibility | Open Your Hard Reset · see proof for each change |
| **Proof** (2–10 min) | Case files matched to their symptoms: result → layers → decisions | HRC has solved *this* before, and how they think | Conviction, respect | "+ Add to your Hard Reset" · Decision Trail · switch lens |
| **Inspection** (technical) | X-Ray: components, data, performance, privacy | The craft is real and the system is clean | Trust, professional respect | Talk to the engineer · forward to the team |
| **Ownership** (Your Hard Reset) | Their File: layers, labels, unknowns, linked evidence | What's known, assumed and missing, and what to do first | Ownership, clarity | Save · download summary · share · send |
| **Handoff** | A "What HRC will see" preview, minimal fields, an honest response window | Exactly what's shared and what happens next | Safety, control | Send · book directly · keep it private |
| **After** | The Reply: a named human's notes on *their* File | A person thought about their company | Being seen, anticipation | Book the call · reply · upgrade to the Diagnostic |
| **Failure states** | 404 = "this page needs a hard reset" (hold to rebuild the route); AI unknown = "We don't know this yet. Ask a person." | The system is honest when it fails | Amusement, trust | Recover in one action |

### 9.4 Visual system: "Paper and Void" [NEW, resolves V1's three palettes]
- **Two surfaces with meaning:**
  - **Paper** = *what it is*: reading, evidence, humans.
  - **Void** = *how it works*: X-Ray, System View, under the hood.
  - Switching surface *means* going deeper.
  - The default is paper: light, editorial and human. That is distinctive against the dark-neon look common to developer-tool and AI startups.
- **Dark-mode preference is honoured** with a dark *reading* theme, kept distinct from X-Ray. X-Ray adds the grid, outlines and telemetry type, so meaning never depends on colour alone (WCAG 1.4.1).
- **The confidence grammar (a signature visual language):**
  - solid = fact
  - tick = evidence
  - dashed = inference
  - lime fill = recommendation
  - hatched = hypothesis
  - open bracket = unknown
  - Readers can *see how sure HRC is*, and the marks are recognisable without a logo.
- **Colour meaning, fixing V1's collisions:**

  | Token | Meaning | Rule |
  |---|---|---|
  | Ink (to define) | Text on Paper | ≥ 4.5:1 on Paper |
  | Paper `#F4F1E8` | Reading surface | Default |
  | Void `#071014` | Inspection surface | X-Ray / System View |
  | Signal lime `#C8FF2E` | **Action and live state only.** One per view | On Paper, only as a fill behind Ink. It measures 1.04:1 as text on Paper |
  | Data cyan `#52E5FF` | Data and links | On Void only. Define a darker variant for Paper (≥ 4.5:1) |
  | Judgement violet `#8A70FF` | **Human annotation and judgement** (the Reply, practitioner notes) | 3.18:1 on Paper, so marks only; a darker variant for text |
  | Labs | Experimental | **Not a hue:** an "EXPERIMENTAL" stamp plus a hatch pattern. This ends V1's violet collision (p.15 vs. p.27) |

- **Three typographic voices:**
  - **Monument:** grotesk headlines, the claim
  - **Telemetry:** mono for data, labels and states
  - **Annotation:** a humane, legible italic for human notes; the voice of the people
  - Together: system, data, human.
- **Recurring objects that make the brand recognisable without a logo:**
  - the hold ring
  - the five-layer exploded stack
  - the confidence marks
  - revision stamps
  - the Reset receipt (§15)
- **Imagery:**
  - real artifacts and real people at work
  - no stock photography and no abstract 3D blobs
  - every diagram labelled. V1's unlabelled lines (p.5, p.12, p.16) break its own rule that "lines appear only when a relationship matters" (p.26).
- **Emotional range: precision + warmth + wit.**
  - Humour is welcome in microcopy and failure states, never in claims.
  - Vulnerability shows through:
    - revision stamps, e.g. "Revision 2 — our first approach failed because…"
    - named practitioners
    - "What we'd do differently"
- **Avoid:**
  - glow as a default
  - film grain
  - hologram clichés
  - constellations as decoration

## 10. Motion + Interaction Philosophy

**Rules**
1. **Motion shows causality.** Every movement shows where something came from, where it went, and why. If the still frame says the same thing, don't animate.
2. **Objects never teleport.**
   - A node in the Tangle, the Reset, a case and the Reset File is the *same object*.
   - Use shared-element transitions: the View Transitions API where supported, a FLIP fallback elsewhere.
3. **Foundations first.**
   - Every rebuild assembles in this order: infrastructure → data → automation → interface → brand.
   - The motion *states HRC's philosophy*.
4. **The feel of the Hard Reset:** tension, stillness, snap, settle. Proposed values; tune them in the prototype.

   | Phase | Duration | Behaviour | Meaning |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | Press | 0 ms | The ring starts; tangle lines start to tighten | "You're in control" |
   | Hold | ~1.2–1.5 s | The ring fills and noise drops. **Releasing cancels with a gentle spring back, no penalty** | Anticipation; commitment |
   | Power-down | ~150 ms | Stillness and dimming: the reboot's black screen | The old system is off |
   | Rebuild | ~600–900 ms | Layers assemble bottom-up, staggered (V1's *Assemble*) | Foundations first |
   | Snap + settle | ~200 ms | Critically damped lock; the diff highlights fade in (V1's *Snap*) | Decision confirmed |
   | Report | — | A text summary in a live region | What changed, in words |

5. **Timing scale:**
   - micro-interactions: 100–150 ms
   - state changes: 200–350 ms
   - transformations: about 1 s at most
   - only the Hard Reset may run longer
6. **Physics:**
   - Structure moves with critically damped springs: confident, no wobble.
   - Only "live" signals (V1's *Pulse*) may overshoot slightly.
   - **Evidence never bounces.**
7. **One primary motion at a time.** Ambient motion stops within 5 seconds or offers a pause (WCAG 2.2.2).
8. **Always interruptible.** Never block input; protect INP.
9. **Confidence moves too.**
   - When an item's status goes up (inference → validated), it **inks in**: the dashed outline becomes solid.
   - Downgrades are never playful.
10. **Scroll reads; it never drives.**
    - No scroll-jacking.
    - Scroll-linked reveals happen only *within* a case's layers.
11. **Never require a particular input method:**
    - hover is never required
    - touch and keyboard have parity
    - every drag has a single-pointer alternative (WCAG 2.5.7)
12. **Reduced motion = the same story in stills,** like a sequence of comic panels. It is not "less brand".
13. **Sound is opt-in and off by default:**
    - a three-part reset signature: power-down tone, silence, boot chord
    - never autoplayed (V1, p.10)
14. **Haptics are a bonus:**
    - an optional pulse at hold start and at the snap, where the Vibration API exists
    - Safari on iOS doesn't support it (verify current status)
    - haptics never carry meaning on their own
15. **Motion is written in code, not mockups.**
    - Durations, easings and spring constants are tokens.
    - Design and engineering co-own them, and development extends the design rather than reproducing it.
16. **The deletion test:** if removing an animation loses no information and no feeling tied to an outcome, delete it.

## 11. AI Experience Strategy

### 11.1 The principle
- **AI composes; it never narrates facts.**
- Humans and structured data state facts. AI selects, arranges, translates and labels, using approved components and content.

### 11.2 Where AI belongs

| Job | When it runs | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Map free-text intent → symptoms and dimensions | Live, on the site | Shows its match and asks the visitor to confirm; deterministic fallback |
| Sequence evidence for a Reset | Live, on the site | Approved objects only; says *why* each was shown; undo |
| Summarise Scan results in plain language | Live, on the site | Restricted to measured fields; scanned text treated as untrusted |
| Draft the Reset File summary | Live, on the site | Every sentence carries a label and a source ID |
| Discovery-call briefing for HRC staff | Internal | Can speculate more, but labelled; never shown to the visitor unedited |
| Turn delivery artifacts into graph objects (the evidence flywheel) | At authoring time | A human approves everything |
| Draft role summaries (Lens) and alt-text | At authoring time | A human approves everything |
| Aggregate patterns across Resets | Internal | Consent, minimum group sizes, sample sizes always stated |

### 11.3 Where AI must never appear
- **Results and metrics.** Numbers are rendered from evidence records, never generated.
- **Pricing, commitments, timelines and guarantees.**
- **Client names or claims** that haven't been approved.
- **A persona:** no name, avatar or "Hi! I'm…". Don't make it feel like a person.
- **Harsh judgements** of a prospect's business, or any legal, financial or regulated claim.
- **Silent decisions.** Every adaptation is explained and can be undone.
- **The first five seconds and accessibility-critical paths.** These are deterministic, fast and cacheable.
- **Covert lead scoring.** Qualification uses only declared or consented data.

### 11.4 Engineering hard rules
- **No generated numbers:**
  - a validator rejects AI output that contains digits in claim text
  - all numbers come from structured Outcome fields (p.11)
- **Structured output only:**
  - output is JSON referencing approved component IDs and content IDs
  - no free HTML
  - unlabelled sentences are dropped
- **Untrusted input:** retrieved CMS text (V1, p.14) **and scanned third-party pages** are treated as untrusted data.
- **Fallback:** if AI times out or fails, the symptom picker and rules carry on and nothing breaks.
- **Release gates (an evaluation suite):**
  - groundedness
  - label accuracy
  - refusal behaviour
  - latency
  - cost
- **Cost and abuse controls:**
  - a per-session token ceiling
  - cached common compositions
  - rate limits and bot protection
- **Order of build:** rules first; add AI only where evaluation shows it beats the rules (V1, p.28).

  - (all per the companion review, §4d)
- **Structured data:** schema.org types for organisation, services, articles/case studies and breadcrumbs.
- **Machine legibility:** clean semantic symptom pages make HRC quotable by answer engines (see §15, idea 1).

### 13.5 Integrations and API boundaries
- **CMS:** headless, with a typed schema including epistemic labels, depth fields, role summaries and claim expiry.
- **CRM and DBR/Nexus:**
  - the Reset File creates or updates an opportunity
  - Reply status syncs back
  - the API contract needs an inventory first
- **Analytics:**
  - privacy-first
  - events keyed to *state transitions* (V1, p.20)
  - no third-party advertising tags (X-Ray would expose them)
- **Email:** magic links and transactional messages.
- **Other services:**
  - PDF rendering
  - an LLM gateway
  - a Scan worker (isolated network, egress rules)
  - feature flags
  - real-user performance monitoring (RUM)

### 13.6 Security and privacy
- **Reset Files hold business-sensitive information:**
  - encrypted at rest
  - unguessable IDs
  - expiry dates
  - delete on request
  - a stated retention period
- **Scan:**
  - blocks internal IP ranges and cloud metadata endpoints
  - rate limits
  - bot protection
  - untrusted-content handling for the AI
- **Legal:**
  - a data-processing agreement with the LLM vendor
  - consent jurisdiction (GDPR / CCPA) defined, which V1 never mentions
- **"What this site knows about you" + delete** is a visible privacy promise. Honour it technically.

### 13.7 Maintenance, versioning, testing, deployment
- **Versioning:**
  - the Reset File schema: files created under v1 must render under v2
  - the content graph
  - the component schemas the AI composes from
- **Tests:**
  - visual regression on state transitions
  - automated and manual accessibility checks
  - end-to-end tests of the Reset flow
  - the AI evaluation suite
  - performance in CI
  - contract tests on the CRM adapter
- **Deployment:**
  - preview environments for content changes
  - feature flags
  - kill switches for WebGL, AI and Scan
- **Ongoing cost:** the main upkeep is the symptom map and the evidence. The flywheel and claim expiry (p.21) keep it current.

### 13.8 Cost drivers
- **Variable:**
  - LLM tokens
  - Scan compute
  - **human reply time**, the biggest. Cap and limit it by fit.
- **Fixed:**
  - writing the symptom map
  - case production
  - accessibility and device testing
  - hosting

### 13.9 Team shape [ASSUMPTION]
- **A V2 core of 3–4 people**, instead of V1's eight roles:
  - a product/strategy lead
  - a design-engineer, who owns motion in code
  - a content and evidence lead
  - a part-time integration/backend engineer
- Accessibility and privacy keep their stop-ship authority, via a reviewer.

### 13.10 Build order

| Order | Build | Why first |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Symptom map v0 + one flagship case file + 3–5 light Manual-format cases + concierge Reply | Content and process are the real risk; tests them without code |
| 2 | Prototype: Tangle + Hold to Reset + X-Ray This Page | The signature, tested with buyers |
| 3 | Semantic shell: Index, case pages, symptom pages, migration + 301s, CMS, analytics, CRM | The platform everything rests on |
| 4 | Reset File v1 (summary, save, PDF, send, CRM record) | The product |
| 5 | Reply at scale, Role Lens, AI intent mapping (only once evaluations pass) | Proven extensions |

### 13.11 Where V2 itself could fail (the skeptic turned on this document)
- **Unlock 1 assumes visitors will describe their business.**
  - Many won't.
  - The demo must persuade with no input at all.
- **The Reply** fails if capacity runs out. A missed promise is worse than no promise.
- **The Scan** could read as surveillance or arrogance. Make it opt-in, keep the tone kind, and keep results private.
- **A Paper default** could read as "less tech". Test it with the target buyers; if it fails, Void becomes the default and Paper stays the reading surface.
- **The symptom map** could balloon into the ontology project V1 warned about (p.11). Cap v0 and grow it from data.
- **Hold to Reset** could become a meme without meaning, if the reset diff isn't real.

## 15. What HRC Hasn't Thought of Yet

1. **Your buyer's AI agent is your next visitor.**
   - Publish HRC's evidence graph as a public, read-only, machine-readable surface:
     - structured pages
     - a manifest file for AI crawlers (an emerging "llms.txt"-style convention)
     - potentially an MCP endpoint
   - Then assistants researching vendors can quote HRC's capabilities, symptoms and *approved* evidence accurately, with citations.
   - This is "an interface category not yet invented" (Lens 03), and it is already arriving.
2. **Case studies that stay alive.**
   - Outcomes re-verified at 3, 6 and 12 months, with stamps such as "Result still holding as of [date]", or honestly, "Declined after 9 months; here's why."
   - Almost no agency shows long-term results.
   - V1's claim expiry (p.21) makes it possible.
3. **Honest disqualification.**
   - Publish who HRC is *not* for, and suggest alternatives.
   - Saying no publicly is one of the strongest trust signals a services firm can send.
4. **The Reset receipt.**
   - A printable, mono-type "receipt" of the visitor's Reset:
     - what was found
     - what's suggested
     - what's unknown
     - the date and an ID
   - Tangible, shareable, pin-up-able, and recognisable without a logo.
5. **The Manual in print.**
   - The paid Diagnostic is delivered as a physical, printed Disassembly Manual of the client's business.
   - A physical object in a digital-only category becomes a memory and a talking point in the boardroom.
6. **"State of the Reset."**
   - Quarterly research built from aggregated, anonymised, consented Reset data, for example "the most common root cause in owner-led companies this quarter."
   - It serves PR, SEO, sales content and offer design.
   - Rules: minimum group sizes, sample sizes always stated, no extrapolation.
7. **The site that keeps its own scorecard.**
   - A public, live panel showing:
     - HRC's own Core Web Vitals
     - accessibility audit date
     - evidence audit date
     - **median time to reply to an inquiry**
   - The Hard Reset philosophy, applied to HRC itself.
8. **Build HRC's own Hard Reset in public.**
   - Document the making of this site in Labs: decisions, failures, numbers.
   - It becomes Case Study Zero and partly solves V1's "one flagship case" dependency.
   - It may also be the natural story for the Design → Co. naming change.
9. **The multiplayer Reset.**
   - The champion invites the CFO and CTO into the same Reset File. Each adds symptoms in their own lens.
   - HRC sees where the buying committee *disagrees*, which is the most valuable discovery insight there is.
10. **Ask the people who built it.**
    - Every case names its practitioners and offers "Ask them a question".
    - The question goes to a human, who answers asynchronously.
    - It adds humanity and gives expert-to-expert access.
11. **Open-source the method.**
    - Release the five-layer Hard Reset framework and symptom map as a free, printable canvas for workshops.
    - Giving the method away builds category authority; the *execution* stays the product.
    - "Hard Reset" becomes industry vocabulary.
12. **Failure states as brand moments.**
    - The 404 page is a broken route you hold to reset.
    - AI "unknown" states are honest and human.
    - A maintenance page shows the system rebuilding.
    - The brand is most memorable where other sites are most generic.
13. **Privacy X-Ray as a public promise.**
    - "Here's everything this site knows about you. Delete it."
    - A one-click demonstration of the ethics behind the systems HRC builds for clients.
